## Evidence checked

The four packs use checksummed source snapshots, have unique fact IDs, no derived facts, and keep source units, dimensions, periods, geography labels, row references, and status flags. The revised Eurostat briefs are version `2.0.0` and match the EU-only packs; the original country-comparison premises were not accepted. The current generation manifest says `source_mode: verified_snapshots`: local HTTPS refresh of Eurostat failed certificate validation, so the pipeline reused previously fetched verified-HTTPS snapshots with matching hashes. This is a reproducible **snapshot review**, not a claim that the live Eurostat API was freshly fetched during this review. No TLS validation was disabled.

- `cb-001` has 6,476 source observations for 1990–2025. The 2024 slice has 194 source entities, while 2025 has only 22; these counts include aggregates. A preliminary three-letter-code check gives 175 entities paired between 2000 and 2024, but that is **not** a validated country count. A country allowlist and an exclusions report must establish the real cohort before any distribution or percentage-point change is accepted. [OWID's indicator definition and upstream ITU/World Bank attribution](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-of-individuals-using-the-internet) also mean the measure concerns recent Internet use, not connection quality.
- `cb-002` has 20 selected EU27 observations: overall (`REN`), electricity (`REN_ELC`), transport (`REN_TRA`), and heating/cooling (`REN_HEAT_CL`) for 2020–2024. The shared latest complete year is 2024. Eurostat describes a break between 2020 (RED I) and 2021 (RED II), and the four sector indicators have distinct denominators. Therefore Step 8 can calculate only **within-category** 2021-to-2024 differences. It must not average or add those four percentages. [Eurostat methodology](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/nrg_ind_share_esmsip2.htm).
- `cb-003` has eight EU27 annual-average HICP rates, 2018–2025, all with `freq=A`, `unit=RCH_A_AVG`, `coicop18=TOTAL`. A fall in this positive rate is not itself a fall in price levels; adding annual rates is not a valid cumulative-price formula. Eurostat distinguishes index levels from monthly, annual, and moving-average rates and identifies household final monetary consumption expenditure as the target universe. [Eurostat HICP metadata](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/en/prc_hicp_esms.htm).
- `cb-004` contains all four reviewed land-cover series for the source-provided World code `OWID_WRL` in both selected five-year windows. The chart itself identifies the World geography and 2002–2025 period. Its NASA MODIS-based historical estimates may under-detect burned area and occurrences, so a calculated change is descriptive, not a statement about all fire activity. The four selected categories must not be represented as an exhaustive burned-area total. [OWID chart and source note](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/area-burned-wildfires-by-type); [GWIS reuse notice](https://gwis.jrc.ec.europa.eu/about-gwis/data-license).

The reviewed inventory marks the four factual inputs as commercial use, modification, and raw-data redistribution `Allowed`, and requires attribution. That evidence is a **candidate snapshot**; `asset_id` remains unresolved (`public_route_only`). It is not a fresh per-asset legal check or proof that permissions cannot change. Eurostat's general commercial reuse policy expressly has third-party and geographic exceptions, so Step 8 stays confined to the reviewed EU27 aggregates. Any later publication must recheck the exact assets and source provenance. [Eurostat reuse policy](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/help/copyright-notice).

## Calculation contract for Step 8

1. Read `data/editorial/analysis-decisions.json` and reject any brief/revision, status flag, dimension, unit, or geography not covered there. Do not infer a wider scope from the larger raw packs.
2. Keep each derived value linked to all input fact IDs, a versioned named formula, the selected cohort and period, the original unit, displayed unit, rounding rule, and a missing/status policy. Calculate from unrounded values; only then round for display.
3. Implement deterministic tests for the permitted formulas and for blocked cases: aggregate accidentally entering a country cohort; missing endpoint; altered Eurostat category/dimension/flag; 2020–2021 renewable comparison; missing wildfire window year; zero denominator in a relative change.
4. For `cb-001`, first create a versioned, cited country-code reference and classify **every** source entity as country, aggregate, or unresolved. Unresolved entities cannot enter the cohort. Confirm at least 150 verified countries with both 2000 and 2024 values. If this gate fails, write a hold result rather than a country analysis.
5. For `cb-002`, keep the four 2024 percentages side by side with their distinct meanings. Compute 2021-to-2024 percentage-point changes only within the same `nrg_bal` category; do not cross the 2020/2021 break.
6. For `cb-003`, use the annual-average rates as supplied. A 2024-to-2025 difference, if shown, must be labelled as a change in the **rate**, in percentage points. Do not infer a price index or cumulative inflation from these facts.
7. For `cb-004`, use only the World facts and compute each category's two five-year arithmetic means if every required annual value exists. The absolute and relative changes compare those means, not two isolated years. Preserve zeros and reject missing values. Include the satellite-detection limitation wherever a result is later used.
8. Keep the creator guide on hold. The checklist's prepared state is not a passed browser, rights, attribution, or calculator-function test.

Step 8 is an offline calculation/evidence implementation, not article writing.
